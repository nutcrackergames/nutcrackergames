Space facts to know while you play

  • Distance to the Sun: Earth is about 150 million kilometres from the Sun. Sunlight takes a little over 8 minutes to reach us.
  • The Moon: it orbits Earth at an average distance of about 384,400 kilometres, which light covers in just over a second.
  • Year lengths: Mercury goes around the Sun in only 88 Earth days, while Earth takes about 365 days.
  • The giant planet: Jupiter is more than ten times wider than Earth.
  • Mars: home to Olympus Mons, a volcano roughly 22 kilometres high, about two and a half times the height of Mount Everest.
  • The space station: the International Space Station circles Earth about every 90 minutes at roughly 28,000 kilometres per hour.

How orbits work

Anything in orbit is falling around a planet instead of falling into it. Gravity pulls the object toward the planet, but the object is also moving sideways so fast that it keeps missing. Go too slow and you fall inward. Go too fast and you drift outward. That balance between gravity and speed is what keeps the Moon around Earth and Earth around the Sun.

It is also why space travel is more about timing and speed than about pointing a rocket at a destination.
