The stages of a flight

  1. Taxi: the plane moves from the gate to the runway.
  2. Take-off: engines at full power, the plane speeds down the runway and the pilot lifts the nose at the right speed.
  3. Climb: the plane gains height after take-off.
  4. Cruise: level flight at altitude, the longest part of most real flights.
  5. Descent and approach: the plane slows and lines up with the runway.
  6. Landing: the wheels touch down, and the plane slows to a stop.

How planes fly

Four forces act on every aircraft. Lift holds it up, weight pulls it down, thrust from the engines pushes it forward, and drag slows it down. A plane flies steadily when these are balanced.

Pilots steer with three main controls: the ailerons on the wings roll the plane left and right, the elevator on the tail pitches the nose up and down, and the rudder turns the nose left and right. Flaps on the wings add lift at low speed, which helps with slow take-offs and landings.

Tips for smoother flights

  • Make small, gentle inputs. Big sudden movements make a plane hard to control.
  • Watch your speed on landing. Too fast and you will not stop in time. Too slow and the plane can drop.
  • Line up with the runway early, so the final approach is a straight, steady line.
  • Level out gently just before touchdown so the wheels meet the runway softly.

This is a game for fun. Real aircraft are far more complex, and the game is not meant for pilot training.
