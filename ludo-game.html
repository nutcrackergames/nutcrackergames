About Ludo

Ludo is one of the best-loved family board games in the world. It comes from Pachisi, a race game that has been played in India for centuries, and the version called Ludo was patented in England in 1896. The idea has barely changed since: roll a die, move your tokens, and try to get home before everyone else.

It is easy enough for kids to learn in a minute, but luck and strategy keep it exciting for adults too.

How to play Ludo

These are the standard rules. Small details can differ between versions, and the game shows its own rules as you play.

  1. Each player has four tokens that start in their coloured yard.
  2. Players take turns rolling the die. In the usual rules you need a 6 to bring a token out of the yard onto the track.
  3. Move a token forward by the number you rolled, going around the board.
  4. If you land on a square holding an opponent's token, that token is sent back to its yard. Squares marked with a star are safe.
  5. Rolling a 6 normally gives you another turn.
  6. Take your tokens up your own coloured column into the centre. The first player to get all four tokens home wins.

Strategy tips

  • Get tokens out early. Having more than one token in play gives you more choices every turn.
  • Use the safe squares. A token on a star square cannot be captured, so park there when opponents are close behind.
  • Watch the six squares behind you. A single roll of the die can reach any square up to six steps away, so a token with an opponent just behind it is at real risk.
  • Know when to hunt and when to run. Capturing an opponent's token costs them all of its progress, but racing your own tokens home matters just as much.
